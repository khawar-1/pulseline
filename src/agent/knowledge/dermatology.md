# Dermatology lead playbook

## The one thing that matters: cosmetic vs. medical intent

Nearly every scoring and outreach decision in derm follows from this split, and
the two behave in opposite ways.

**Cosmetic** (botox, fillers, laser hair removal, cosmetic peels)
- Self-pay, so insurance is irrelevant and never a blocker.
- Highly deadline-driven — weddings, reunions, holidays, vacations. A patient
  who names a date has effectively pre-committed.
- Price-sensitive and comparison-shopped. A lead that asks only about price with
  no date attached is usually shopping several practices.
- Books fast when a date exists; evaporates when it does not.

**Medical** (mole and skin checks, acne, rashes, psoriasis, biopsies)
- Insurance status is the gating factor. In-network is a strong positive signal.
- Referrals and post-biopsy follow-ups are near-certain bookings — the decision
  to be seen was already made elsewhere.
- Duration matters: a concern the patient describes as persistent or worsening
  over weeks converts far better than a brand-new one, because they have already
  waited and decided it is not resolving.

## Scoring signals

**Raises the score**
- A named date or event (cosmetic) — the single strongest signal in derm.
- Existing referral, prior biopsy, or transfer of care from another dermatologist.
- Self-pay declared on a cosmetic inquiry (removes all insurance friction).
- In-network insurance named explicitly on a medical inquiry.
- Both phone and email present.
- Open or flexible scheduling availability.

**Lowers the score**
- Price-only inquiry with no stated timeline or reason.
- Missing phone number — derm coordinators book by phone, and email-only leads
  convert materially worse.
- Very narrow availability (single weekday evening, Saturdays only) against a
  practice that mostly runs weekday daytime hours.
- Vague reason for visit with no duration and no specifics.

## Typical booking window

Cosmetic consults book 1–2 weeks out. Medical visits 2–4 weeks, unless a
referral or prior biopsy is involved, in which case the practice usually
prioritises them into earlier slots.

## Outreach guidance

- **Cosmetic:** lead with the date the patient named and confirm there is time
  before it. Consultations are the natural first step, so offer one rather than
  discussing treatment.
- **Medical:** offer the earliest opening and confirm network status. Do not
  characterise what they described (see `compliance.md`, Rule 1) — the pull to
  say "that's worth getting looked at" is strong here and it is still clinical
  interpretation.
- **Missing phone on a medical lead:** the message's actual job is to collect a
  phone number, not to book. Ask for it directly.
